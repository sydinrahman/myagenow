How Generations Are Calculated: Gen Z, Millennials, and Gen Alpha Timelines

By the myagenow team • 7 min read • Updated October 2026

Are you a Millennial or Gen Z? Is your little cousin Gen Alpha? And who decided any of this? The truth is a bit messier than most charts let on. Here are the birth years, the reasoning behind them, and a quick way to find your own generation.

The Quick Answer

These are the birth-year ranges most commonly used in the United States, based on Pew Research Center for the older groups. The age column shows the age you turn in 2026 (your birth year subtracted from 2026), so your actual age depends on your birthday.

Generation Birth years Age turning in 2026
Silent Generation 1928–1945 81–98
Baby Boomers 1946–1964 62–80
Generation X 1965–1980 46–61
Millennials (Gen Y) 1981–1996 30–45
Gen Z 1997–2012 14–29
Gen Alpha* 2010–2024 2–16
Gen Beta* 2025–2039 0–1 (so far)

*Pew has not published official dates for Gen Alpha or Gen Beta. The ranges above come from the research firm McCrindle. Many other sources start Gen Alpha in 2013 instead. More on that below.

Who Decides Where Each Generation Starts and Ends?

Nobody, officially. There’s no government office or scientific board that stamps a birth year onto a generation. The labels come from researchers, demographers, journalists and marketers, and they borrow from each other until a few ranges become the standard. Pew Research Center’s version is the one you’ll see cited most often for Boomers through Gen Z.

The lines are usually drawn around shared moments: a major event, an economic shock, or a technology that changed daily life while a group of people was growing up. That’s why you’ll often hear that Millennials came of age around the turn of the millennium, while Gen Z has never known a world without smartphones.

Why the spans are about 15 or 16 years

Look at the table and a pattern jumps out. Gen X, Millennials, and Gen Z each cover 16 years. Boomers stretch to 19 because of the post-war baby boom. McCrindle goes even tidier, with 15 years per generation. Real life isn’t that neat, but a roughly generation-length span keeps the groups comparable and easy to remember.

Keep in mind: generations describe big groups, not individuals. Someone born in 1998 and someone born in 2011 are both “Gen Z” on paper, and they can have very different childhoods.

How to Work Out Your Generation (Step by Step)

  1. Find your birth year. The month and day don’t matter here. Only the year does.
  2. Match it to a range. Use the table above.
  3. Check for a cusp. If you were born within a year or two of a boundary (1964–65, 1980–81, 1996–97, 2012–13), you might feel like you belong to both neighbors, and some sources would put you on the other side of the line.

If you also want to know how old a generation is today, the math is one line:

Age this year = current year − birth year
(subtract 1 if your birthday hasn’t happened yet)

Example: someone born in 1996, the last Millennial birth year, turns 30 in 2026 (2026 − 1996 = 30).

Millennials: 1981–1996

Pew defines Millennials as people born from 1981 through 1996. They’re also called Gen Y, and in 2026 they’re between 30 and 45 years old. The oldest are heading toward 50 faster than they’d like to admit, and the youngest have just turned 30.

They sit in an interesting spot: old enough to remember a childhood before the internet was everywhere, young enough to have adopted it quickly as teens and young adults.

Gen Z: 1997–2012

Pew’s range for Gen Z is 1997 to 2012, which makes them 14 to 29 in 2026. It’s the generation that grew up with smartphones, social media, and, for the older half, a young adulthood interrupted by the pandemic.

Not everyone agrees on the edges. Some argue the oldest Gen Z were born as early as 1995, and other sources push the end date a year or so later. If you were born in 1996 or 1997, don’t be surprised if you see yourself on both lists.

Gen Alpha and Gen Beta: The Moving Target

This is where the dates get slippery, because the youngest generations are still being counted.

  • McCrindle: Gen Alpha is 2010–2024, and Gen Beta covers 2025–2039.
  • Other sources: many start Gen Alpha in 2013, right after Pew’s Gen Z cutoff, and end it around 2024 or 2025.
  • Pew: has not set official dates for either group.

The practical result is an overlap. Someone born in 2011 could be called Gen Z (by Pew’s range) or Gen Alpha (by McCrindle’s). Neither is wrong. They’re just using different rulers.

Gen Alpha is often described as the first generation born entirely in the 21st century, and the first to grow up with tablets, voice assistants, and AI tools as everyday objects.

Cusp Generations and Micro-Generations

When a boundary is arbitrary, people on either side of it tend to create their own names. You may have heard these:

  • Xennials: people born roughly in the late 1970s to early 1980s, between Gen X and Millennials.
  • Zillennials: people born around the mid-to-late 1990s, between Millennials and Gen Z.

They aren’t official categories. They’re a handy way to say “I don’t quite fit the standard label,” and the exact years vary depending on who you ask.

Frequently Asked Questions

What years are Millennials born?
Pew Research Center defines Millennials (Gen Y) as individuals born from 1981 through 1996. In 2026, Millennials reach ages 30 to 45.

What years are Gen Z born?
Gen Z covers birth years 1997 through 2012 based on Pew Research Center boundaries. In 2026, members of Gen Z are turning 14 to 29 years old.

What years is Gen Alpha?
According to the demography research firm McCrindle, Gen Alpha covers birth years 2010 through 2024. Other research sources start Gen Alpha in 2013 (the year after Pew's Gen Z cutoff) through 2024 or 2025.

Who decides where one generation ends and the next begins?
There is no official government agency or official board setting generation boundaries. Dates are established through consensus among demographers, social researchers (like Pew Research Center), marketers, and media outlets tracking social, cultural, and technological milestones.

Am I a Millennial or Gen Z if I was born in 1996 or 1997?
In Pew's widely accepted framework, 1996 is the last year of Millennials and 1997 is the first year of Gen Z. Born right on the boundary, you share characteristics of both groups and are often categorized under the "Zillennial" micro-generation.

Final Thoughts

Generations are handy shorthand, but they’re conventions, not science. The edges move, the sources disagree, and the newest labels are still being worked out. Use the birth years here as a solid starting point, and don’t sweat it if you land on a boundary. You’re in good company.
