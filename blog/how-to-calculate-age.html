Calculating your age seems straightforward when you simply count the number of birthday anniversaries you have celebrated. However, finding your exact age in completed years, months, and remainder days requires taking into account varying month lengths and leap years.

What You Need to Get Started

To calculate your age manually or programmatically, you need two exact calendar dates:

  • Date of Birth (DOB): The day, month, and year you were born (e.g., June 15, 1998).
  • Target Date: Today's current date or a specific future/past date (e.g., October 3, 2026).

The Step-by-Step Age Formula

Under standard Gregorian calendar rules, exact age is computed by breaking down the date components from largest to smallest unit:

Step 1: Calculate Completed Years

Subtract your birth year from the current target year. If your birthday has not yet occurred in the current target year, subtract 1 from the total years.

Step 2: Calculate Remaining Months

If the target day is greater than or equal to your birth day, subtract the birth month from the target month. If the target day is less than the birth day, borrow 1 month from the month count and add the number of days in the preceding month.

Step 3: Calculate Remainder Days

Subtract the birth day from the target day. When borrowing days from the previous month, adjust for whether that specific month contained 28, 29, 30, or 31 days.

Example Calculation

Date of Birth: June 15, 1998
Target Date: October 3, 2026
Result: 28 Years, 3 Months, and 18 Days.

Why Manual Calculations Get Tricky

Doing calendar math manually becomes complex because months do not have an equal number of days. February has 28 days (or 29 in a leap year), while April, June, September, and November have 30 days, and the remaining seven months have 31 days.
