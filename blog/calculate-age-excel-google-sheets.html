How to Accurately Calculate Someone's Age in Excel or Google Sheets

By The My Age Now team · Published October 27, 2026 · 8 min read

You have a column of birth dates and you need ages. It sounds like a one-minute job, and it usually is, until you notice that half your results are off by one. Someone turning 30 tomorrow shows up as 30 today, or a person who just had a birthday is still listed a year younger.

The good news is that the right formula is short. The bad news is that the most obvious formula is wrong, and one of the "official" options has a bug Microsoft itself warns about. Here's what actually works, in both Excel and Google Sheets.

The formula most people try first (and why it fails)

The instinct is to subtract the birth date from today and divide by 365:

=(TODAY()-A2)/365

This breaks because a year isn't 365 days. Leap years add an extra day every four years, so over 30 years you're already about a week off. Switching to 365.25 helps, but it still produces wrong answers for people whose birthday is within a few days of today.

Age is a calendar question, not a division problem. You want to know how many full birthdays have passed, and there's a function built for that.

The reliable way: DATEDIF

DATEDIF counts the complete years, months or days between two dates. With the birth date in cell A2:

=DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y")

That returns age in whole years, and it only goes up on the actual birthday. It works in Google Sheets and in every modern version of Excel.

One quirk: Excel doesn't list DATEDIF in its autocomplete or function wizard. It still works, so type it out yourself and don't assume it's broken when the suggestions don't show it.

The third argument decides what you get back:

Code What it returns
"Y" Complete years between the dates
"M" Complete months between the dates
"D" Total days between the dates
"YM" Months left over after complete years
"MD" Days left over after complete months (see the warning below)

Getting years, months and days together

To show something like "24 years, 7 months, 12 days", you can combine three calculations in one cell. This version is safe in both Excel and Google Sheets:

=DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y") & " years, " & DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "YM") & " months, " & (TODAY() - EDATE(A2, DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "M"))) & " days"

The first two parts are straightforward. The day count needs a bit more explanation.

Don't use the "MD" option

You'll see plenty of tutorials use DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "MD") for the leftover days. Microsoft's own documentation warns that this option can return negative numbers, zero, or a wrong result in some situations.

The formula above avoids it. EDATE moves the birth date forward by the number of complete months, and subtracting that date from today leaves the exact number of days remaining. It's a few more characters, but you can trust the answer.

Using a date other than today

Sometimes you don't want today's age. Maybe you need someone's age on the first day of school, at the date of an event, or on a given cut-off date. Put that date in its own cell, say B2, and swap it in for TODAY():

=DATEDIF(A2, B2, "Y")

This also stops your results from changing every time you open the file, because TODAY() recalculates constantly. For reports you plan to keep, a fixed date is the safer choice.

Age as a decimal

If you need a figure like 24.6 for analysis, use YEARFRAC:

=YEARFRAC(A2, TODAY(), 1)

The 1 tells it to use actual days in each year. Wrap it in INT() if you want whole years, though DATEDIF is usually the cleaner choice for that. Use YEARFRAC when you want the decimal itself.

Next birthday and days until it

Here's a handy extra. To find the next birthday:

=EDATE(A2, 12 * (DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y") + 1))

And to count the days remaining:

=EDATE(A2, 12 * (DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y") + 1)) - TODAY()

Format the first cell as a date and the second as a plain number.

Calculating a whole column at once

In Excel, write the formula in the first row and drag the fill handle down, or double-click it to fill automatically.

In Google Sheets you can do the whole column with one formula and skip the dragging:

=ARRAYFORMULA(IF(A2:A="", "", DATEDIF(A2:A, TODAY(), "Y")))

The IF part keeps empty rows blank instead of throwing errors.

The February 29 trap

People born on February 29 are where age formulas get interesting. In a non-leap year there's no February 29, so when does their birthday happen?

There's no universal rule. Some places and tools treat it as February 28, others as March 1. Spreadsheet functions follow their own logic, and DATEDIF generally counts the new year of age on March 1 in non-leap years. If your data includes leap-day birthdays and the exact day matters, for example for legal or eligibility purposes, test a few cases in your own sheet and decide which convention you need. Don't assume the spreadsheet matches another tool.

Common problems and quick fixes

  • The result shows as a date like 1/0/1900. The cell is formatted as a date. Change the format to General or Number.
  • You get #VALUE!. The birth date is probably stored as text. Check whether it's left-aligned, which usually means text. Re-enter it, or convert it with DATEVALUE.
  • You get #NUM!. The start date is later than the end date. Check for typos in the year.
  • Days and months look swapped. This is a regional setting issue. A date like 03/04/2000 means March 4 in the US and April 3 in much of the rest of the world. If you import data from elsewhere, check how your spreadsheet reads it.
  • Ages change when you reopen the file. That's TODAY() doing its job. Use a fixed date cell if you need a snapshot.

FAQ

What's the best formula to calculate age in Excel?

=DATEDIF(A2, TODAY(), "Y") for age in whole years. It counts completed years and only increases on the birthday.

Does DATEDIF work in Google Sheets?

Yes, with the same syntax as Excel.

Why is dividing by 365 wrong?

Leap years mean a year averages about 365.25 days, so the result drifts, and it's often off by one right around a birthday.

How do I calculate age on a specific date?

Replace TODAY() with the cell holding that date, for example =DATEDIF(A2, B2, "Y").

Can I calculate age for a whole list of people?

Yes. Fill the formula down in Excel, or use ARRAYFORMULA in Google Sheets.
